1 Executive summary — the 60-second view

If a steering-committee member reads nothing else on this page, this is the slide they get. Business problem, current health, my Test Manager read, decisions I need, and where the evidence lives.

1 · Business problem & release objective

The regulator has mandated compression of the overnight reconciliation window by Q2 2027, and the legacy COBOL/Oracle payment core is on vendor runout with no defect-fix path. The programme replaces it with a cloud-native payments platform (Java + Postgres + Kafka, ISO 20022) and moves ops to Salesforce Service Cloud, delivered as pilot on 2027-01-24 and GA on 2027-02-21 — 11 two-week sprints end-to-end.

2 · Programme health AMBER

On schedule — one specific exposure

Scope: 48 stories across 4 epics, 2 sprints closed, 9 to go. Primary exposure: UAT environment instability — 2 outages last sprint, ~1.5 days of lost test execution.

  • SDET staffing: 1 open seat (plan = 4 QA incl. me)
  • Compliance sign-off path TBD on 1 of 7 payment flows
  • Pilot cohort selection draft complete; central-bank schema v3 pending
3 · Test Manager assessment

Amber-conditional-Green, not Red

Delivery against baseline is on track. The risk I’m carrying is downstream regression confidence, driven by environment stability — not velocity, not defect density. Today’s evidence supports a conditional go on the Sprint 8 regression gate, upgradable to a full go if D1 below lands by end of Sprint 3.

2 My decisions as Test Manager — 3 worked scenarios

A dashboard reports; a Test Manager decides. Three scenarios that would land on this programme — each one with the signal, my analysis, the options I’d put to leadership, my recommendation, and the owner it goes to. All hypothetical.

Scenario 1 UAT environment instability threatens Sprint 8 regression readiness

Signal

2 UAT outages in Sprint 2 · ~1.5 days of test execution lost · pass-rate dropping on the shared environment. Environment-health card is Red.

Analysis

Infrastructure, not defect density. The Sprint 8 regression suite needs a stable UAT by Sprint 7 to produce defensible exit evidence. Shared environment contention is the root cause.

Options
  • A — Dedicated UAT environment ($18K/qtr). Preserves Sprint 8 confidence.
  • B — Keep shared + 20% regression buffer, prioritize risk-based first.
  • C — Defer; accept conditional go at pilot gate.
Owner & deadline

Decision owner: Head of Platform Engineering + Programme Director.
Deadline: End of Sprint 3 (2026-11-01).
Escalation trigger: UAT stability not demonstrated by Sprint 5.

My recommendation

Option A. $18K/qtr is below the cost of a 1-sprint GA slip. If declined, I will formally lower the Sprint 8 exit statement to "conditional go, risk owned by Platform Engineering" and re-plan the regression sequence to protect the top-5 payment flows at the expense of the long tail.

Scenario 2 Reconciliation discrepancies remain before pilot

Signal

In Sprint 8, 3 unresolved net-position mismatches above the 0.01% tolerance band. Reconciliation tile shows variance Red for 2 consecutive runs.

Analysis

Two are transformation-rule gaps the team can close in Sprint 9. One is a central-bank schema disagreement (ownership outside the team) — it will not close on our calendar.

Options
  • A — Hold pilot until all 3 close.
  • B — Pilot with cohort exclusion of the affected payment type; close the external item in parallel.
  • C — Pilot full scope; set a hard rollback trigger on the central-bank variance.
Evidence to proceed
  • Signed exception register (per excluded payment type).
  • Rollback drill completed in Sprint 10 within RTO 2h / RPO 15min.
  • Central-bank engagement log showing weekly contact + target close date.
My recommendation

Option B. Hard-scope the pilot cohort. Full pilot (Option C) leaks risk I don’t own; holding (Option A) blocks on an external item we can’t accelerate. Decision owner: Programme Director + Compliance.

Scenario 3 AI-assisted test selection reduces execution time but risks missing defects

Signal

The risk-based-selection agent cuts the CI gate from 42 min to 24 min on an evaluation set of 180 historical PRs containing 50 known real defects (plus 130 clean PRs). Measured recall on the real-defect denominator: 47 of 50 = 94% (3 missed real defects, all non-P1).

Analysis

A 6% miss at PR level is tolerable when the full suite still runs nightly. At the release gate the same miss rate is not acceptable — one leaked P1 at pilot could cost the GA date.

Options
  • A — PR level only; mandatory full suite at release gate.
  • B — All gates; auto-disable if nightly misses > 1 real defect / month.
  • C — Advisory only; agent suggests, human still runs full suite.
Guardrails (whichever option)
  • Eval set versioned and reviewed every 2 sprints.
  • HITL overrides logged with reason; feed back into the eval set.
  • One-switch disable at CI level.
  • Release gate always runs the full suite until 3 consecutive pilots are clean.
My recommendation

Option A + all 4 guardrails. Measurement: monthly precision/recall vs the versioned eval set, published on the AI QE Agents dashboard. Owner: SDET Lead + Dev Lead. Rollback triggers: severity-weighted miss score > 6 on the eval set, OR > 2 real defects leaked in a rolling 30-day window, OR a single P1 leak. The AI selection runs at PR level only; the release gate always runs the full suite.

3 The programme behind these dashboards

Every dashboard on this page is a slice of one hypothetical delivery. Imagining a single, end-to-end programme is what forces each metric to earn its place — because an unanswered question somewhere else is a defect waiting to be shipped.

Business situation

Legacy state. A regional bank runs its core payments ledger on a 22-year-old COBOL + Oracle system. Operations run cases from an in-house ticketing tool. Settlement is batch, overnight; reconciliation to the central bank is partially manual. Regulator has flagged the reconciliation window as a systemic risk and set a 12-month deadline.

Target state. A cloud-native payments platform (Java + Postgres + Kafka) speaking ISO 20022, with Salesforce Service Cloud as the agent desktop for operations. Settlement moves from batch to near-real-time with intra-day reconciliation. AI augments triage, data validation and release decisions — as explicit, auditable agents, not black-box automation.

Why now. Regulatory deadline, 25% cost base locked in legacy mainframe licensing, and no vendor-side defect fix path for the current switch.

Banking core ISO 20022 Salesforce Service Cloud Data migration Legacy COBOL/Oracle Regulatory reconciliation AI QE pod

Why this makes a good dashboard case study

A banking modernization touches every surface a Senior Test Manager owns in a steering review: data integrity (migration + reconciliation), functional risk (payment correctness, rollback), operational risk (Salesforce case flow + agent desktop), release risk (pilot → GA ramp) and programme risk (velocity, defect aging, environment health). The same twelve dashboards you need here are the twelve you need on any modernization — so building them against this story keeps them honest.

6 Testing types needed across the programme

What the 3 QA + 1 SDET Lead would run, mapped to the dashboards that would show the evidence. "Done" isn’t a test type — it’s a dashboard reading "green for N consecutive sprints".

DMData migration validation

Source profiling, record-count reconciliation per table, field-level diff on primary + risk columns, transformation-rule assertions.

  • Row count exact; business-key uniqueness
  • Checksum per risk column (amount, BIC, status)
  • Null-ratio drift < 0.5%

RCReconciliation (end-to-end + intra-day)

Net-position reconciliation between core ledger, payment switch and central-bank sandbox; signed CSV; tolerance 0.01%.

  • Hourly intra-day job; T+0 close
  • Variance > tolerance opens a case automatically
  • AI diff narrator explains the mismatch

RBRollback / DR drills

Point-in-time restore of Postgres ledger, cross-check against legacy Oracle snapshot, dual-run cutback runbook.

  • Scheduled drill at Sprint Demo240
  • Rollback RTO < 2h; RPO < 15 min
  • Automated reconciliation check post-restore

FCFunctional & contract

ISO 20022 schema contract tests; Salesforce case-flow BDD; payment correctness including reject paths.

  • Contract tests gate every PR
  • End-to-end payment: happy, reject, timeout, duplicate
  • Case SLA assertions on routing

PFPerformance & chaos

Baseline at 1x expected volume in Sprint Demo236 (Sprint 5); 2x by Demo238 (Sprint 7); 3x by Demo240 (Sprint 9) so the pre-pilot sign-off pack is complete. Chaos: broker loss, dead-letter spike, DB failover.

  • p95 latency < 600ms at 3x
  • No message loss on broker loss
  • Automatic failover within 30s

SESecurity & compliance

Threat model refresh; SAST/DAST in CI; audit of PII on new paths; penetration test pre-pilot.

  • Zero critical SAST findings on main
  • Pen test closed before Sprint Demo242
  • Audit trail for every reconciliation decision

PRPilot release (canary)

Feature-flagged ramp 5% → 25% → 50% → 100% over Sprint Demo242; auto-rollback on error-budget burn.

  • Error budget: 0.3% of pilot volume
  • Cohort selection excluded high-value accounts in week 1
  • Rollback trigger wired to Pilot Rollout dashboard

FBFeedback loop

Pilot survey weekly, NPS from agents (Salesforce desktop), operations stand-up readout; telemetry on top-5 agent flows.

  • Agent NPS > 30 by end of pilot
  • Top-5 case-resolution time ≤ legacy
  • All open feedback items triaged within 48h

How "reconciliation" and "rollback" actually surface to the steering committee

Reconciliation. The intra-day job writes a signed CSV. The Reconciliation dashboard shows net position by rail, variance against tolerance, and the top-5 unresolved mismatches with the AI diff narrator’s explanation attached. Steering sees one number — "variance within tolerance for 14 consecutive runs" — not the CSV.

Rollback. We don’t promise a flawless cutover; we promise a credible cutback. The Pilot Rollout dashboard shows the rollback RTO/RPO measured in the Sprint Demo240 drill, the time it took, which runbook step was slowest, and the owner for the next drill. Steering sees "drill completed within RTO, 1 action" — and asks about the one action.

Pilot & feedback. Pilot ramp percent, error-budget burn, agent NPS and the top-5 feedback themes are the same card, so there’s no "green on numbers, red on sentiment" blind spot.

7 Programme governance beyond test metrics

Test metrics alone don’t ship a banking release. These three views cover the non-test levers a Test Manager owns the conversation on: dependencies on the critical path, the sign-off layers between “QA green” and “Go-Live approved”, and the recovery options when a gate slips.

A · Dependencies & critical path

A dependency becomes a defect the moment it’s late. Each one has an owner outside the delivery team and a sprint we need it by.

  • Regulator sandbox access — needed by Sprint 4; owner: Compliance Liaison. Slip impact: intra-day reconciliation evidence delayed by 1 sprint.
  • Central-bank schema v3 — needed by Sprint 6; owner: Central-bank liaison. Slip impact: see Scenario 2 (cohort exclusion).
  • Salesforce production org provisioning — needed by Sprint 8; owner: Salesforce admin + Procurement. Slip impact: pilot cohort reduced to internal only.
  • Legacy data snapshot refresh — needed weekly from Sprint 5; owner: DBA. Slip impact: reconciliation false positives.
  • Dedicated UAT environment — see D1 above; owner: Head of Platform Engineering.

B · Business acceptance & sign-off layers

“All tests pass” is not a release decision for a payment platform. Five layers, each with its own evidence pack and its own sign-off.

  1. QA sign-off — SDET Lead. Evidence: regression + risk-based + automation pack.
  2. Business UAT acceptance — Business Owner. Evidence: sampled payment set + agent-flow sign-off.
  3. Operational readiness — Ops + SRE. Evidence: runbook drill + on-call rota + chaos results.
  4. Security & compliance — CISO + Compliance Officer. Evidence: SAST/DAST clean, pen-test closed, audit trail per decision.
  5. Final release authority — Programme Director + Head of Payments. Evidence: all 4 above + the Executive summary above.

C · Forecast confidence & recovery options

If a gate slips, the last conversation to have is “we missed”. The first one is “here are the levers”.

  • Lever 1 — Defer lower-risk tests. Impact: coverage drops ~8% on long-tail flows; top-5 unaffected.
  • Lever 2 — Reallocate SDET capacity from EPIC-QE to EPIC-PR. Impact: AI pod work pauses for 2 sprints; manual regression stays at parity.
  • Lever 3 — Narrow pilot cohort. Impact: pilot evidence quality down; GA confidence hold.
  • Lever 4 — Request 2-week GA slip. Impact: regulator engagement; cost of 1 vendor-licence cycle; team morale.
  • Not an option — Skip the regression gate. Named explicitly, so it is not quietly chosen.

🔍 AI evaluation example — risk-based test selection (QE-06)

Positioning: the AI-assisted suite is an additional, faster PR feedback mechanism — not a replacement for the full-suite release gate. The release decision never rests on the AI selection alone.

10 What’s inside each dashboard

For the tiles that don’t yet have a live sample page, this is the field-level specification — the one question it answers, the KPIs it tracks, and where AI plugs in.

🎯 Test Strategy

Answers: How are we testing this programme, and why?

The strategy card has one tab per layer (unit, API/contract, UI, data, perf, security, chaos) with a per-sprint entry/exit, tool, environment and owner. The acceptance gates (functional, data integrity, performance, operational readiness) are visible with target thresholds, not vague adjectives.

  • Coverage by layerTarget vs actual coverage %, trend over 4 sprints.
  • Test type mix% unit / contract / E2E / manual; alarm if E2E > 25%.
  • Environment uptimeStaging + regulator sandbox uptime, data refresh SLA.
  • Gate stateEntry/exit per gate, who’s the sign-off.
🤖 AI: the test-case drafter proposes scenarios from BRDs; QA accepts, edits or rejects with a reason that feeds back into the agent’s eval set.

💵 ROI Calculator

Answers: What did QE automation save this programme?

Open formulas, not black boxes. Each metric shows "(cost today) vs (cost without the automation / without the AI agent)" using sprint-level data. The AI pod has its own cost line — the ROI number is net of agent spend.

  • Manual-hours avoidedBaseline: run time before automation. Per sprint, split by automation vs AI pod.
  • Cycle-time deltaPre-release regression hours; before / now / target; source: CI run logs.
  • Pre-prod catch rateDefects caught before UAT / defects found before + after UAT, same cohort.
  • $ / defect avertedIllustrative; formula and inputs visible; hypothetical, labelled.
  • Payback periodFor AI pod cost (compute + eval-set labour); break-even sprint.
  • Velocity (for ROI context)2-sprint actual + 1-sprint forecast, labelled explicitly — not a 3-sprint rolling actual until Sprint 4 completes.
🤖 AI: risk-based test selection shaves ~40% off gate-time on the evaluation set without losing coverage — the dashboard reconciles the two numbers so steering doesn’t have to. All AI savings are shown as estimated unless a measured sprint-over-sprint delta is available.

🤖 Automation Effort

Answers: Where is automation effort landing, and is it paying off?

Keeps the team honest about new vs maintenance. A sprint where 70%+ of QA time went to maintenance is a flag, not a surprise. Every KPI shows its denominator and scope beside the number — cumulative regression automation % and sprint-level automation progress use different populations and must never share a cell.

  • New vs maintenance %Denominator: QA hours logged this sprint. Scope: per QA, per epic.
  • Flake rateDenominator: total runs in the window. Target < 1.5%; shown per suite.
  • MTTR for broken testsMedian + p90; scope: in-scope automation suites only.
  • Pass-rate stabilityRolling 10-run pass rate; scope: full suite vs risk-based subset labelled separately.
  • Cumulative regression automation %Denominator: all in-scope regression cases to date. Separate from the sprint-level number.
  • Sprint-level automation progressDenominator: automation stories planned this sprint. Separate from the cumulative number.
  • Suite runtimeFull suite + risk-based smoke subset; label which gate each one runs at.
🤖 AI: the triage agent reclassifies failures as flake vs real-defect vs environment, feeding the flake-rate number automatically; the reclassification is reviewed every 2 sprints by a human.

🛡 Release Readiness (Go / No-Go)

Answers: Can we ship? What’s open?

Four cards — Functional, Data, Performance, Operations — each with a RAG, an owner, a cite-your-evidence link, and a "what would make this red" sentence. Updated T-2 days before every steering committee. The dashboard makes the distinction between a provisional Test Manager recommendation and a formal release authorization unmistakable — a conditional go is not a go.

  • Gate RAGFunctional / Data / Perf / Ops; each with evidence link.
  • Recommendation vs authorizationTwo separate signals: TM recommendation (provisional) and formal release authority sign-off (final).
  • Open P1/P2With age bucket and owner.
  • Rollback drill freshness< 1 sprint old at pilot; target drill in Sprint Demo240 (Sprint 9) — matches the sprint cadence and the testing-types card.
  • Pilot error-budget burnOnly visible during Sprint Demo241 (Pilot); 0.3% of pilot volume.
  • DependenciesExternal sign-offs waiting (regulator, vendor) — owners named.
🤖 AI: log clustering surfaces "this incident looks like class X we closed in Sprint Demo238" so the gate conversation is grounded.

🐞 Defect Analytics

Answers: Where do defects come from, and where are they stuck?

Every metric here carries a definition, denominator, time window, source of truth, target and owner. A metric without those cannot be defended in a steering committee.

  • Aging matrixSeverity × age-bucket; target per bucket; source: defect tracker; owner: QA Lead.
  • Injection vs detection phaseShift-left evidence; measured on closed defects only; window: cohort-locked at release.
  • Defect densityDefects / 1k lines-changed, per module; outliers highlighted; window: last 4 sprints.
  • Reopen %Reopened defects / closed defects in the same cohort; rolling 4 sprints; target < 5%.
  • DRE (Defect Removal Efficiency)Numerator: defects found pre-release in a cohort. Denominator: pre-release + post-release defects in the same cohort. Observation window: 60 days post-release. Target: > 90%.
  • Defect leakagePost-release defects / pre-release defects, same cohort, 60-day window. If pre-release = 0, show N/A and report the absolute post-release count separately — never divide by zero.
  • Top-5 themesClustered from defect bodies by the triage agent; weekly; human-approved before publishing.
🤖 AI: clustering produces the top-5 themes; log-RCA agent attaches a candidate root cause to each new P1 for human confirmation.

⚠ Risk Register

Answers: What could stop us, and who owns it?

  • Top-15 risksProbability × impact (1-5 each), owner, mitigation.
  • Mitigation freshnessDays since last update; flag > 14.
  • Stale-risk flagRisks unchanged in 3+ steering reviews.
  • Realized risksRisks that became incidents, with before/after cost.
🤖 AI: the diff narrator from the reconciliation job writes an auto-draft risk whenever variance breaches tolerance — a human owner confirms it before it lands on the register.

📊 Reconciliation Dashboard

Answers: Did the right data land, and does it still balance?

  • Row-count deltaPer table source → target per batch.
  • Field-level diff %On risk columns (amount, BIC, status, dates).
  • Variance vs toleranceNet position per rail per hour (tolerance 0.01%).
  • Time to resolve mismatchMedian + worst-case.
  • Signed-pack freshnessLast signed CSV to regulator sandbox.
🤖 AI: the diff narrator reads a variance and produces a 3-line explanation, with linked payment IDs and rule references. Humans decide, but on signal, not noise.

🚀 Pilot Rollout

Answers: How is the canary going, and can we roll back?

  • Ramp %5 / 25 / 50 / 100 cohort state.
  • Error-budget burnBudget 0.3% of pilot volume.
  • Cohort compositionBy segment; high-value accounts excluded week 1.
  • Agent NPSWeekly; target > 30.
  • Rollback RTO / RPOFrom last drill; target 2h / 15min.
  • Open feedback themesTop-5 with owner + due sprint.
🤖 AI: log cluster + RCA agent for pilot incidents, always with human sign-off before customer comms.

⚡ Non-functional (Performance & Security)

Answers: Will it stay up and stay safe at pilot scale?

  • Latency p95 / p99At 1x / 2x / 3x expected volume.
  • Throughput headroomPeak tested vs expected pilot peak.
  • Chaos resultsBroker loss, dead-letter spike, DB failover.
  • SAST / DAST openCriticals + highs with age.
  • Pen-test stateOpen findings pre-pilot.
  • Audit trail completeness% of reconciliation decisions with a trace.
🤖 AI: log clustering correlates perf dips with deployment events; useful at the 2x / 3x step where root cause is rarely obvious.
